## Pin Bar Definition

A pin bar is a candlestick pattern characterized by "a long upper or lower wick, also called a shadow or tail." The defining feature requires "a wick that is at least two-thirds of the candlestick's range," with the body remaining relatively small.

Key components:
- **Tail:** At least 2/3 of the entire bar length
- **Body:** Open and close positioned close together, near the nose
- **Nose:** Small relative to the tail (not always strictly required)

The tail represents rejection of a price level — price moved strongly in one direction but was rejected and closed near the opposite end of the range.

## Two Pin Bar Types

**Reversal Pattern:** Forms at range highs/lows or pullbacks within larger trends, signaling potential directional shifts.

**Continuation Pattern:** Forms in the direction of established trends with minimal pullback, indicating momentum persistence.

## Entry Methods

### Method 1 — Break of Pin Bar Nose

Placing stop orders "just beyond the nose" (typically 10–20 pips away) for conservative entries. Provides lower risk-reward multiples but higher fill probability.

### Method 2 — 50% Retrace Entry (Preferred)

Using Fibonacci retracement tools to enter at the **50% level** of the pin bar tail-to-nose distance. This approach "increases the potential R-multiple considerably" but carries the risk of unfilled orders if price does not retrace sufficiently.

## Stop Loss Placement

"The best place to set your stop loss on a pin bar trade is above or below the pin bar tail," with 10–20 pips additional buffer recommended based on trading comfort and currency pair characteristics.

Stop losses placed at the end of the tail with buffer protect against premature stop-outs from wicks while keeping risk defined.

## Profit Targets

Identify support and resistance levels on the chart, placing take-profit orders at the "first area of support or resistance." This conservative approach prevents overexposure while capturing reliable gains.

## Confluence Requirements

Pin bar validity increases significantly with multiple confirming factors:

1. **Well-formed pattern at key levels** — prior swing highs/lows, round numbers
2. **Alignment with underlying trend direction** — trade with-trend pins > counter-trend
3. **Dynamic support/resistance interaction** — moving averages (20 EMA, 50 EMA)
4. **Adequate room for price movement** — no immediate resistance directly above (bullish pin)
5. **Higher timeframe alignment** — daily pin aligns with weekly trend

## Inside Bar Pin Bar Combination

This advanced pattern combines an inside bar with a subsequent pin bar, requiring:

1. **Daily timeframe only** — does not work reliably on intraday charts
2. **Key support/resistance level** — pattern must form at a meaningful level
3. **Pin bar close contained within inside bar range** — or strong close showing momentum

This combination delivers higher win rates than individual patterns alone due to the double consolidation signal before the breakout.

## Risk Management

- Minimum acceptable risk-to-reward ratio: **1:3**
- Author targets **1:5 or better** setups for optimal profitability
- Never risk more than 1–2% of account per trade
- If confluence factors are missing, skip the setup

## Key Principles

- The tail is the most important element — it must be at least 2/3 of the total range
- Context (trend, key level, momentum) matters more than pattern perfection
- Daily and 4-hour timeframes produce the most reliable signals
- Patience to wait for high-confluence setups is the primary edge
