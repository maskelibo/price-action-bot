## Overview

Grimes introduces the concept of volatility compression, explaining that markets operate in two primary modes. In **mean reversion mode**, extreme price movements quickly reverse as traders buy dips and sell spikes, creating rangebound conditions. Conversely, **range expansion mode** features directional movements fueled by feedback loops that drive predictable price action.

## Measuring Volatility

The author defines volatility simply as "the range covered in an average daily session," using **Average True Range (ATR)** as the measurement tool. This includes gaps from the previous day's close, providing a more complete picture of actual market movement.

## The Compression Indicator

Grimes presents a chart of E-mini S&P 500 futures showing a red line representing the **ratio of 5-day to 60-day ATR**. When this line falls below 1.0, short-term volatility has contracted relative to longer-term conditions — a signal that directional moves often emerge.

The article examines four marked examples on the chart:

1. A failed trade with no strong follow-through
2. A successful setup leading to a two-week decline after a trend day
3. A trend day without multi-day extension
4. An ongoing compression area

## Trading Application

"Do not be buying weakness or shorting into strength" during compression phases. Instead, traders should position themselves to participate in emerging moves rather than fade them. Grimes recommends holding long exposure while remaining flexible to trim positions if weakness develops.

## Reference Works

Grimes references similar volatility-based approaches in works by:
- **Toby Crabel** — classic volatility contraction/expansion research
- **Linda Raschke** — momentum and volatility trading methods

He notes his forthcoming book (The Art and Science of Technical Analysis) would explore range expansion studies following compression in greater depth.

## Key Takeaways

- Low short-term volatility relative to longer-term baseline signals potential directional breakout
- ATR ratio (short-period / long-period) below 1.0 = compression state
- Compression does not guarantee direction — confirms only that a significant move is possible
- Trade with the emerging direction; do not fade during compression
