## Overview

Support and resistance represent foundational concepts in technical analysis, reflecting where supply and demand forces create price equilibrium zones.

## Support Defined

"Support is the price level at which demand is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from declining further."

As prices fall toward support:
- Buyers become motivated to purchase (value perception improves)
- Sellers hesitate (price already low)
- These forces create a floor that resists further decline

## Resistance Defined

"Resistance is the price level at which selling is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from rising further."

As prices rise toward resistance:
- Sellers become eager to sell (value perception decreases)
- Buyers lose interest (price already high)
- These forces create a ceiling limiting upward movement

## Establishing Support and Resistance

### Highs and Lows Method

- **Previous reaction lows** establish support
- **Previous reaction highs** establish resistance

These historical price points represent where buyers and sellers previously engaged, and that memory persists in market psychology.

### Support Equals Resistance Principle (Role Reversal)

When prices break below support, that level can transform into resistance. Similarly, when prices break above resistance, it becomes new support. This reflects shifted supply-demand dynamics:

- Former support buyers become breakeven sellers (now resistance)
- Former resistance sellers become trapped buyers seeking to exit (now support when broken above)

### Trading Ranges

Prices moving within narrow bands indicate balanced forces. Breakouts above or below these ranges signal that one side has overwhelmed the other.

### Support and Resistance Zones

Rather than exact price points, **zones** work better for analyzing large trading ranges spanning months. This acknowledges that technical analysis isn't perfectly precise — levels are areas, not lines.

## Key Principle

"If a support or resistance level is broken, the relationship between supply and demand has changed."

Breaks indicate fundamental shifts in market psychology and participant behavior. The significance of the break increases with:
- Volume on the breakout
- Duration of time price spent in the prior range
- Number of touches of the level before the break

## Practical Application

1. Mark prior swing highs as resistance candidates
2. Mark prior swing lows as support candidates
3. Note where price has reversed multiple times (stronger levels)
4. Watch for role reversal after decisive breaks
5. Use zones rather than exact prices for entry/stop decisions
