## Part 1: Candlesticks and Support

### Overview

Individual candlesticks and their patterns function as indicators when price action interacts with support levels, whether establishing new support or confirming existing ones.

### How Candlestick Patterns Interact with Support

**Bullish Reversal Patterns**

Patterns like Bullish Engulfing, Hammer, and Morning Star indicate that "buying pressure overcame early selling pressure for a strong finish." These reversals suggest sufficient buying demand exists to establish meaningful support.

Key bullish reversal patterns at support:
- Hammer
- Bullish Engulfing
- Morning Star
- Piercing Pattern

**Increased Buying Pressure Signals**

The Inverted Hammer and Long White Marubozu demonstrate sustained buying activity. These patterns show "increased buying pressure rather than an actual price reversal," signaling buyer strength near support zones.

**Neutral Indecision Patterns at Support**

Doji and Spinning Top formations represent market uncertainty but "denote indecision and are generally considered neutral." However, they signal "a decrease in selling pressure" — after a decline, a pause in selling pressure can still mark support level formation.

### Real-World Examples

- **EDS**: A Doji and Spinning Top confirmed support at $58 after a prolonged trading range
- **Broadcom (BRCM)**: A Bullish Engulfing pattern validated new support around $208
- **Medtronic (MDT)**: Multiple patterns (Spinning Top, Harami, Hammer) reinforced support at approximately $46

### Trading Application

The methodology combines:
1. Identify the support zone first (prior lows, round numbers, moving averages)
2. Wait for a bullish candlestick signal at that zone
3. Require confirmation (next bar, volume, or additional pattern)
4. Enter with stop below the support zone

## Part 2: Candlesticks and Resistance

### Overview

Single candlesticks and candlestick patterns serve as tools to confirm or identify resistance levels. These can mark new resistance after price advances or validate existing resistance within trading ranges.

### Bearish Reversal Patterns at Resistance

Bearish reversal candlesticks indicate that "buying pressure was suddenly overturned and selling pressure prevailed." This rapid shift suggests overhead supply may establish a resistance level. Key patterns:

- Bearish Engulfing
- Bearish Harami
- Dark Cloud Cover
- Evening Star / Bearish Abandoned Baby
- Shooting Star
- Three Black Crows

### Increased Selling Pressure Signals

The Hanging Man, Long Black Candlestick, and Black Marubozu demonstrate elevated selling activity:
- Hanging Man: "a long lower shadow indicates intra-session selling pressure"
- Black candlesticks: "sustained selling pressure that moved prices significantly lower"

This intensity suggests weakening buyer conviction and potential resistance formation.

### Indecision Patterns at Resistance

Doji and spinning tops "indicate decreased buying pressure but no noticeable increase in selling pressure." The stalemate they represent signals potential resistance without strong reversal confirmation — useful as early warning signals.

### Real-World Examples

Case studies with Veritas, Lucent, and Delta Air Lines demonstrating how candlesticks confirmed resistance levels before significant price reversals.

### Trading Application (Resistance)

1. Identify the resistance zone (prior highs, round numbers, moving averages)
2. Wait for a bearish candlestick signal at that zone
3. Require confirmation (gap down, high volume, additional pattern)
4. Enter short with stop above the resistance zone

## Combined Framework: Confluence Trading

The most robust setups combine:
- Pre-identified support/resistance zones
- Candlestick pattern signals at those zones
- Momentum indicator confirmation (RSI overbought/oversold, MACD cross)
- Volume confirmation (above-average volume on reversal bar)

This multi-confluence approach reduces false signals and improves the probability of successful trades.
