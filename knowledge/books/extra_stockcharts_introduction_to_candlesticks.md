## History of Candlestick Charts

Japanese traders employed technical analysis for rice trading during the 17th century. While differing from Charles Dow's later American approach (circa 1900), both systems shared core principles:

- Price action matters more than underlying reasons
- Markets reflect all available information
- Psychological factors (fear and greed) drive trading
- Markets naturally fluctuate
- Actual prices may diverge from intrinsic value

According to Steve Nison's research, candlestick charting emerged after 1850, with much credit attributed to Homma, a legendary rice trader from Sakata. His initial concepts underwent refinement across decades of trading practice, eventually becoming the modern candlestick system.

## Formation of Candlestick Charts

Creating candlesticks requires datasets containing "open, high, low and close" values per time period. The hollow or filled section comprises the **body**, while thin lines above and below represent high/low ranges called **shadows** (or wicks and tails).

- **Hollow candlesticks**: Form when closing prices exceed opening prices (bullish)
- **Filled candlesticks**: Form when closing prices fall below opening prices (bearish)

Traders prefer candlestick charts over traditional bar charts for their visual clarity and intuitive interpretation. The open-close relationship carries vital significance: hollow candlesticks signal buying pressure, while filled candlesticks indicate selling pressure.

## Long Versus Short Bodies

Body length correlates with trading intensity. Extended bodies demonstrate powerful buying or selling, whereas short candlesticks suggest minimal movement and price consolidation.

**Long white candlesticks:** Reveal strong buying pressure, with substantial advancement from open to close indicating aggressive buyer participation. After prolonged declines, they may signal reversals or support levels.

**Long black candlesticks:** Demonstrate strong selling pressure, with significant downward movement from open to close. Following extended advances, they may foreshadow turning points or resistance zones.

**Marubozu bars** — candlesticks lacking upper or lower shadows — are the most potent variants:
- *White Marubozu:* Opening equals the low and closing equals the high; complete buyer control
- *Black Marubozu:* Opening equals the high and closing equals the low; complete seller dominance

## Long Versus Short Shadows

Upper and lower shadows provide valuable session insights:
- Short shadows: confined trading action near open-close levels
- Long shadows: extended price movement beyond open-close range
- Long upper shadow + short lower shadow: buyer dominance followed by seller pressure
- Long lower shadow + short upper shadow: seller activity overcome by buyer recovery

**Spinning tops**: Long upper shadows, long lower shadows, and small bodies — representing indecision. After advances or long white candlesticks, signal weakening bullish momentum. After declines, indicate diminishing bearish control.

## Doji

Doji occur when security opening and closing prices are virtually equal, creating cross, inverted cross, or plus-sign appearances. These neutral patterns become meaningful through preceding price action and subsequent confirmation.

Doji convey indecision and price tug-of-war — prices move above and below opening levels during sessions but close near opening points, creating standoffs where neither bulls nor bears gain control.

### Doji and Trend

- Following advances or long white candlesticks: doji signal weakening buying pressure
- After declines or long black candlesticks: doji indicate diminishing selling pressure
- Confirmation is always required; doji alone cannot confirm reversals

### Key Doji Types

**Long-Legged Doji:** Long upper and lower shadows nearly equal in length; significant movement resulting in minimal net change; strong indecision signal.

**Dragonfly Doji:** Opening, high, and close are equal; lows create long lower shadow (T-shape). Indicates seller dominance during sessions with buyer recovery by end. Bullish reversal potential after downtrends.

**Gravestone Doji:** Opening, low, and close are equal; highs create long upper shadow (inverted T). Indicates buyer-driven advance that sellers reversed. Bearish reversal potential after uptrends.

## Bulls Versus Bears

Candlesticks depict bull-bear battles over specific periods. Six primary game types:

1. **Long white candlesticks**: Bulls controlled trading throughout most periods
2. **Long black candlesticks**: Bears controlled trading throughout most periods
3. **Small candlesticks**: Neither team advanced significantly; standoff
4. **Long lower shadows**: Bears controlled early action but bulls mounted impressive comebacks
5. **Long upper shadows**: Bulls controlled early action but bears mounted impressive comebacks
6. **Long upper and lower shadows**: Both sides experienced moments without decisive advantage

## What Candlesticks Don't Tell You

Candlesticks reflect open-close relationships and high-low values but cannot reveal event sequences between opening and closing. Multiple scenarios can produce identical candlestick appearances. Understanding underlying context is essential.

## Prior Trend Requirement

According to Greg Morris's "Candlestick Charting Explained," reversal patterns require preceding trends to reverse:
- Bullish reversals necessitate downtrends
- Bearish reversals require uptrends

"The last 1–4 weeks" of price action typically proves most effective for trend assessment.

## Candlestick Positioning

**Star Position:** Candlesticks that gap away from preceding candlesticks — isolated from previous price movements.

**Harami Position:** Candlesticks forming within previous candlestick bodies. ("Harami" means pregnant in Japanese — the second bar nests within the first.)

## Long Shadow Reversals

Two reversal pattern pairs feature single candlesticks with small bodies, one long shadow, and one short or absent shadow. The long shadow typically measures at least twice the body length.

| Pattern | Context | Signal |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Hammer | After decline | Bullish reversal |
| Hanging Man | After advance | Bearish reversal |
| Inverted Hammer | After decline | Bullish reversal (needs confirmation) |
| Shooting Star | After advance | Bearish reversal |

**Hammer:** Bullish reversal following declines; long lower shadow indicates seller pressure then buyer recovery.

**Hanging Man:** Bearish reversal following advances; long lower shadow confirms seller-driven declines; bulls regained footing by end, raising caution flags.

**Shooting Star:** Bearish reversal after advances; long upper shadow shows intraday rally completely reversed by close.

**Inverted Hammer:** After downtrends; long upper shadow indicates intraday buying pressure; requires bullish confirmation (gap up or long white candle).

## Blending Candlesticks

Multiple candlesticks can combine into single candlesticks:
- **Bullish Engulfing + Piercing Patterns** → blend into hammers
- **Bearish Engulfing + Dark Cloud Cover** → blend into shooting stars
- **Three White Soldiers** → create long white candlesticks
- **Three Black Crows** → create long black candlesticks
