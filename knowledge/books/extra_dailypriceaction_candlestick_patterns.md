## What is a Candlestick Pattern?

A candlestick pattern reflects price movement within a single timeframe interval. The article distinguishes these from **chart patterns**, which develop across multiple candlesticks. Candlestick patterns are single or 2-bar events; chart patterns (head & shoulders, flags, wedges) are multi-bar structures.

---

## 1. The Pin Bar Pattern

**Structure:** The pin bar features a pronounced tail (or wick) representing at least two-thirds of the entire candlestick, a small body showing minimal distance between open and close, and a minimal nose.

**Why Trade It:** Four advantages:
- Easy identification with proper chart setup
- Favorable stop-loss placement opportunities (beyond the tail)
- High profit potential when properly executed
- Most effective on **daily and 4-hour timeframes**

**Application:** The pattern signals potential reversals when formed at key support or resistance levels following extended price moves. It represents market rejection — price was pushed strongly in one direction but reversed convincingly.

---

## 2. The Inside Bar Pattern

**Structure:** The inside bar's range sits entirely within the previous bar's range (the "mother bar"). The inside bar is smaller and contained within the prior bar.

**Key Characteristics:** This pattern functions primarily as a **continuation signal** during established trends, representing consolidation before directional breakouts.

**Trading Rule:** "An established trend is a requirement for trading this particular candlestick pattern" — inside bars indicate consolidation, not reversal. Trading against the trend with inside bars leads to poor outcomes.

**Entry:** Place a stop order just above or below the mother bar to catch the breakout in the trend direction.

---

## 3. The Engulfing Candlestick Pattern

**Structure:** The engulfing bar completely envelops the previous bar's entire range (both body and shadows in the strictest definition; at minimum the body).

**Critical Requirement:** These patterns must form "at a key support or resistance level AND after an extended move up or down" to warrant trading. Without this context, engulfing patterns produce poor results.

**Signal Type:** Engulfing patterns typically indicate **reversals**:
- Bullish engulfing at support → bullish reversal signal
- Bearish engulfing at resistance → bearish reversal signal

**Avoiding False Signals:** "Both candlestick patterns must form at a key level to be tradable, otherwise traders may find themselves trading false positives." This is the most common mistake — trading engulfing patterns in the middle of ranges.

---

## Key Trading Principles

All three patterns require:
1. Formation at significant support/resistance levels
2. Extended prior price movement (setup context)
3. Combination with confluence factors (trend, momentum, key levels)
4. Risk management with defined stop-loss placement
5. Minimum 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio before entry

## Timeframe Guidance

- **Daily timeframe**: Best for all three patterns; reduces noise
- **4-hour timeframe**: Secondary option; more signals, slightly more noise
- **Intraday**: Not recommended for beginners; pattern reliability drops significantly
