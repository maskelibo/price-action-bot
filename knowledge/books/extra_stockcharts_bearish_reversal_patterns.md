## Overview

A bearish reversal candlestick pattern indicates "a potential change from uptrend to downtrend" and signals shifting market sentiment from buying to selling. All patterns require a pre-existing uptrend and subsequent confirmation.

## Key Bearish Reversal Patterns

### 1. Bearish Abandoned Baby (3 Candlesticks)

**Structure:** Long white candlestick → doji gapping above it → long black candlestick gapping below

The gaps are critical — they represent complete breaks between buyer and seller conviction. Considered one of the strongest bearish reversal signals. Additional confirmation usually unnecessary due to gap structure.

### 2. Bearish Engulfing (2 Candlesticks)

**Structure:** White candlestick followed by a large black candlestick that completely encompasses the white body

Selling overwhelms buying after an opening gap up. The engulfing bar's body must exceed the prior bar's body. Stronger when it also engulfs the shadows.

### 3. Bearish Harami (2 Candlesticks)

**Structure:** Large first candlestick with small body second candlestick inside it

Can appear in various color combinations. Effectiveness increases significantly when the second is a **doji** (Bearish Harami Cross). Signals indecision after a strong move.

### 4. Dark Cloud Cover (2 Candlesticks)

**Structure:** White candlestick followed by black candlestick opening above the previous close but closing below its midpoint

Key requirement: the second candle must close below the midpoint of the first. Indicates strong selling pressure overcoming initial strength. Below the midpoint = sellers have majority control.

### 5. Evening Star (3 Candlesticks)

**Structure:** Long white candlestick → small white/black candlestick or doji gapping above → long black candlestick

The middle candlestick signals indecision at the top. When the middle is a doji, the pattern becomes an **Evening Doji Star** (stronger signal). Gap between first and second candlestick strengthens the pattern.

### 6. Shooting Star (1 Candlestick)

**Structure:** Single candlestick with small body, long upper shadow (at least twice body length), minimal lower shadow

Ideally gaps from preceding candlestick. The long upper shadow represents a failed rally — price attempted to extend the advance but sellers overwhelmed buyers and pushed price back near the open.

## Confirmation Requirements

Most patterns require "bearish confirmation" within 1–3 days:
- Gap down
- Long black candlestick
- High-volume decline

The exception is Bearish Abandoned Baby, which self-confirms via gaps.

## Context Requirements

All patterns must form within an existing **uptrend** to qualify as reversals. A bearish engulfing in a downtrend is continuation, not reversal.

## Technical Analysis Integration

Enhance pattern reliability by incorporating:

**Resistance Levels**
- Where price has struggled to advance historically
- Round numbers, prior swing highs, Fibonacci retracements

**Momentum Indicators**
- MACD: Look for bearish crossover or histogram weakening
- RSI: Overbought reading (above 70) + divergence strengthens the signal
- Stochastics: Overbought + bearish crossover

**Volume Indicators**
- OBV (On-Balance Volume): Should show distribution
- Chaikin Money Flow: Negative or turning negative confirms selling pressure

## Pattern Strength Hierarchy

From most to least reliable (all else equal):

1. Bearish Abandoned Baby — strongest (requires gaps)
2. Evening Doji Star — very strong (doji at top)
3. Evening Star — strong
4. Dark Cloud Cover — moderate-strong
5. Bearish Engulfing — moderate
6. Shooting Star — moderate (one bar, needs confirmation)
7. Bearish Harami — weakest (needs most confirmation)

## Key Principle

No candlestick pattern operates in isolation. Context (prior trend, key levels, momentum state, volume) determines whether a pattern carries genuine predictive weight or is noise.
