## Overview

Grimes introduces a novel visualization technique that challenges conventional candlestick pattern analysis. The core demonstration presents identical price action — specifically the afternoon reversal from 5/11/23 in S&P 500 futures using 3-minute bars — rendered with different time offsets. Each variation produces visually distinct candlestick patterns despite representing "precisely the same price dynamics."

## Two Schools of Thought

The article identifies opposing perspectives on price patterns:

**Pattern Significance School:** Practitioners assign considerable meaning to granular details of individual bars and candlestick formations, often assuming other traders use identical tools and timeframes.

**Randomness School:** Acknowledges that markets contain substantial noise and randomness, cautioning that excessive pattern focus may lead traders to perceive meaningful patterns in random data.

## The Core Problem

Grimes emphasizes that candlesticks represent market data rather than defining market reality itself. He writes: "the market exists, doing its thing, and we use the bars on our screen to get a perspective on the market." When identical price action produces drastically different visual patterns depending on bar offset, traders anchoring decisions to specific pattern details risk basing strategies on representational artifacts rather than underlying market conditions.

## Practical Application: Reversal Complexes

Rather than abandoning technical analysis, Grimes proposes a reoriented approach focusing on what price bars reveal about conviction behind moves. He introduces the concept of "reversal complexes" — a term he believes he coined — encouraging traders to examine how bars span timeframes instead of obsessing over specific formations.

## The Unchanging Elements

Despite visual variations across different time slices, certain market characteristics persist:

- **Trend strength indicators** remain consistent
- **Momentum loss signals** (smaller ranges, doji patterns at turns) maintain visibility
- **Significant selling pressure** appears across most timeframe variations
- **Climactic formations** may appear in some panels but not others

## Conclusion

Grimes concludes that aligning chart reading with underlying market truth — rather than pattern minutiae — produces more effective trading frameworks aligned to actual market behavior. The recommendation: shift focus from individual bar shapes to the broader "reversal complex" that spans multiple bars and timeframes.
